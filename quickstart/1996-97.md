# Quick start: 1996–97 (NA8, OBD-II)

One page for this model-year range. The full guide is [INSTALL.md](../INSTALL.md); each step below links to its section. **Nothing here has been run in a car yet.** Steps nobody has checked are marked unchecked in the guide.

Other years: [1989–93](1989-93.md) · [1994–95](1994-95.md) · [1996–97](1996-97.md) · [1999–2000](1999-2000.md) · [2001–05](2001-05.md)

Manual-gearbox cars (and automatics swapped to manual) are the supported ones. **Automatic cars are untested.**

## What is in the box

The ECU, assembled and flashed: the daughterboard soldered to the 1996–97 motherboard, which carries the MAP sensor, the wideband connector and USB-C. If you built your own, do [BUILD.md](../BUILD.md) first.

## What you add

- A **Bosch LSU 4.9** wideband sensor (0 258 017 025), genuine, and **AEM's X-Series sensor harness 30-3427** (or a lead made up to the J2 pin map).
- **Vacuum hose**, 3–4 mm inside diameter, about 3 m, and a **4 mm barbed tee**.
- A USB-C cable and a laptop with [TunerStudio](https://www.tunerstudio.com/).
- A multimeter and a timing light.

Everything is on the [parts page](../additional-parts/shopping_list.html).

## Where the stock ECU is

Behind the passenger seat, under the carpet, on the rear bulkhead. Held by three 10 mm nuts.

Keep it: its case is the new ECU's case, and its board is your way back to stock. **The 1996–97 case fit is unchecked.** The board has the 1990–95 outline and four M4 holes (not six) as a stand-in, and the 76-position harness header is 130 mm wide. Check the fit against your case before cutting anything. See the measuring section below.

## Measure before you remove the stock ECU

**Unchecked: these are the checks the first install will make.** Do them with the stock ECU still in and the car working. The cavity names are the ones in the [1996–97 motherboard README](../motherboards/1996-97/README.md). Write each reading down: some of them decide whether a part has to be added to the board before it goes in.

**(a) Tachometer, cavity 4L.**
- Does the tachometer work now?
- With the engine idling, the voltage on 4L should switch: a meter on DC shows a steady average of several volts.
- Note whether 4L ever reads near 12 V with the engine stopped and the key on.
- Why it matters: this board's tach output is an open-drain driver with no pull-up. If the car's own wiring does not pull the line up, a pull-up to switched 12 V is needed: fit R37 (2.2 kΩ, 1206, left empty at the factory; from motherboard rev 0.15 on). On an earlier motherboard, solder about 2.2 kΩ (¼ W) from 4L to the board's +12 V. A reading near 12 V (key on, stock ECU unplugged) means nothing to add; a reading near 0 V means the pull-up is needed ([bring-up notes](../ordered-boards/2026-11/BRING-UP.md)). Take the reading both with the stock ECU in and, once, with it unplugged.

**(b) A/C request (1K) and clutch switch (1L).**
- Measure the voltage on each with the switch released and pressed (A/C button on and off; clutch pedal up and down).
- The board reads both as switches to ground with 12 V pull-ups (R21, R22). The stock ECU's tables say 12 V (battery) with the switch open and under 2.5 V closed. If either wire goes to 12 V when operated instead, that resistor is wrong for the car: leave it off and set the input back to active high.

**(c) Air temperature, cavity 3K.**
- At about 20 °C the voltage should read about 2.3–2.5 V (the stock manual's figure: about 2.5 V on a 1996, 2.3–2.4 V on a 1997). That also confirms the stock sensor works.

**(d) Vehicle speed, cavity 1M.**
- While the car rolls (drive wheels turning), the voltage should pulse. A meter on DC will show an average that moves with speed; a scope shows the pulses.
- Note whether it swings between about 0 V and a high level, or only pulls down. The pull-up R20 is fitted only if the signal switches to ground or is open collector.

**(e) The case.**
- Photograph the ECU in its bracket, from several sides.
- Measure the case: outer length x width x height, and the screw positions between case and bracket.
- These measurements are for the planned own case, and for finding out whether this board's outline fits the real case.

## Settings for this year

Year button: **1996-97**. There are no jumpers on this board. Harness header: TE 178780-1, 76 positions.

## Gotchas for this year

- **Leave the airflow meter plugged in.** The ECU ignores its airflow signal but reads the air-temperature sensor inside it. The pump needs nothing: the ECU switches it on cavity 1U.
- **The ECU is behind the passenger seat**, so the MAP hose and the wideband lead are long. Run them from the firewall along the passenger sill, under the trim.
- The A/C request (1K) and clutch switch (1L) close to ground. The board pulls both up to 12 V (R21, R22) and the firmware reads them active low. R20, the vehicle-speed pull-up (1M), is not fitted.
- The stock oxygen-sensor heater is driven by the ECU. Leave the stock oxygen sensor's plug unconnected when the wideband is in.
- To take the airflow meter out, see [Taking the airflow meter out](../INSTALL.md#taking-the-airflow-meter-out-optional). Find the two sensor wires with a meter first: guides disagree on this plug.
- Add-ons (boost valve, flex fuel, nitrous, CAN): [section 9](../INSTALL.md#9-add-ons-on-the-add-on-header). On the motherboard they are J5 pins.

## Air temperature and MAP hose

The stock sensor is inside the airflow meter. ECU cavities: signal 3K, ground 3O.

**MAP hose:** take vacuum from a small manifold hose, through the tee. Route the hose along the firewall, then between the intake manifold and the valve cover. Through the firewall by the rubber plug behind the windshield-washer bottle: drill a hole in it just big enough for the hose. Never take it from before the throttle plate, the brake booster or the crankcase breather.

## First-start checks

The full list: [INSTALL.md, First start](../INSTALL.md#8-first-start) and [firmware/README.md, First start](../firmware/README.md#first-start).

1. Key on, engine off: green lights on, no red light. TunerStudio connects. Coolant and air temperature read the weather, MAP reads about 100 kPa, battery reads within half a volt of a meter on the battery.
2. Air temperature, engine cold: within a few degrees of coolant temperature. About -40 °C means an open circuit (an unplugged meter or sensor).
3. Throttle: calibrate closed and wide open.
4. Crank: the fuel pump primes, RPM shows about 200, and the engine starts.
5. Base timing: lock it at 10°, check with a timing light on cylinder 1, correct "Trigger Angle Advance" until the light agrees, then unlock it.
6. Warm it up and watch the wideband: lambda far from 1.0 at idle means stop and find out why. First drives: light throttle only.
