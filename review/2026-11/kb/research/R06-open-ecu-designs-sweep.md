# R06 - Sweep of other open-source ECU hardware designs (2026-11-16)

## Summary
1. Searched GitHub (REST API, ~50 keyword/topic queries, saved in work/R06-.../dl/ghsearch), web search, cloned 13 repos shallowly. Excluding rusEFI/FOME/official Speeduino, the open designs with real hardware files are few: SPECTRE, Blitzbox BL49sp, SCG-ECU 2.0, Speeduino-M5x-PCBs (pazi88), WTMtronics SpeedyBoards (UA4C/NO2C/Ocelot), daveecu2 (Miata, unbuilt?), ECUTeen, RavAGE (FreeEMS, 2013), Route ECU (STM32H743, PCBWay page), community rusEFI PnP adapters. All are hobby-scale; none documents a production-quality qualification (EMC, load dump) - VERIFIED by reading their READMEs.
2. Injector/low-side idiom census: (a) bare logic MOSFET AO3400A (Blitzbox, high-Z injectors only, no visible external clamp; in a car per author README); (b) dual logic MOSFET BUK9K52-60 (SCG-ECU, no visible clamp; bench/engine video claimed); (c) self-protected (clamped, current-limited, thermal) low-side parts: BSP78 (SPECTRE), NCV8408B (M5x PnP), NCV8402/NCV8406 (Ocelot), VNLD5090 (daveecu2). The self-protected parts cost $0.25-1.8 at JLC; AO3400A costs $0.02-0.03.
3. Ignition idiom: every design uses a TC4424/TC4427/MIC4427/IXDN602-class gate driver (or IGBT with ISL9V3040) - none uses the small logic-level-input IGBT-direct approach as rusEFI; no cheaper "free" idiom found.
4. Only two designs ship JLCPCB BOMs with LCSC codes that are directly usable: Blitzbox V0.1.9 (full CSV) and M5x PnP (EasyEDA export); SCG-ECU has an XLS partial BOM (could not be fully parsed here).
5. Honest limit: very few designs are shown to survive in a car long-term; field evidence is in R31.

## Cheaper or simpler idioms, with proof status
1. AO3400A bare low-side for high-Z injectors, 4k7 gate + 100k pulldown (Blitzbox). Proof: author README says V0.1.2 ran on a real car (25.04.2022) and V0.1.9 is "current stable"; no tuned-fleet evidence. It relies on the FET avalanche for flyback (INFERRED; AO3400A is 30 V, so 14-42 V flyback spikes are over its Vds rating, a robustness concern - UNPROVEN long term). Only for low-current high-Z injectors, which a Miata has (stock 12-16 ohm); inductive-kick energy figure not computed.
2. SOT-223 self-protected low-sides NCV8402/8406/BSP78 (SPECTRE, Ocelot): $0.24-0.75 at volume, clamp + current limit + thermal in one part. Proof: SPECTRE author tested 0.5.3; Ocelot is a sold product. Check against what the project uses before swapping (compare with R08).
3. Zero-cost removal candidates seen elsewhere: none of the surveyed designs uses fewer protection parts than a single TVS plus series Schottky/diode - Blitzbox power input is SS54A + SMBJ22A + LM2940 (cheapest observed, ~$0.4 total) and is reported working in cars (README, UNVERIFIED independent).
4. LM2940 LDO in SOT-223 at $0.13-0.20 (Blitzbox, Ocelot LM2940LD) vs buck (SCG LM2596 + inductor). LDO dissipation at 14.4 V x current is a thermal limit; not computed for this project.
5. USB ESD: ESD0504F ($0.08) used by Blitzbox in place of USBUF02W6 ($0.59) (C74936 priced today). Proven on Blitzbox (UNVERIFIED in field).
6. SCG-ECU idiom: STM32F407 + external W25Q16 flash + LPS22HB baro; its SLC-Free wideband (PSoC CY8C24223A) needs a "Cypress chip" not in JLC catalogue (README) - not cheaper/simpler than the project's STM32F103 wideband.

## Implications for this project
- (Cost, medium confidence) Replacing a dedicated smart low-side or MOSFET+clamp arrangement with AO3400A is not proven robust; only adopt with measured flyback. Evidence above.
- (Cost, medium) Blitzbox JLC BOM is a template for basic/extended part choices; compare against kb/parts_manifest for cheaper passive SKUs (C11702, C25744 etc.).
- (Doc/UX) rusEFI-community PnP repos publish gerbers only with notes about placement rotation, supporting the repo's open item about unchecked JLC rotations.
- (Info) No open design with a Miata-specific tested PnP besides rusEFI's own; daveecu2 abandoned 2021.
