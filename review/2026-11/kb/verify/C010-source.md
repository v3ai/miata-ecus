# C010 source check: verdict PARTLY (true as a report; weak as a risk to this design)
Checked: miataturbo threads 107805 (fetched live) and 103610 (saved copy work/R12-how-owners-tune-miatas/blown.html); repo README/INSTALL/VERIFICATION M5.
- 107805: quote confirmed. A DiyAutoTune engineer (relayed by a poster) attributes it to "a ground voltage offset between the alternator and the battery negative" that makes the field "latch" on. He says the ECU "tends to slowly overcharge to 16V+ over a couple hours". Fix: a good ground strap on the alternator body. Battery Voltage Correction (ms/V) is a TunerStudio setting.
- 103610 (2020): boiling battery on a 99 MSPNP2 is true, but the OP's later post (#17) says the cause was the vendor's repaired unit having its regulator set to always output maximum, fixed by the vendor at no cost. This is not a ground offset. Another poster (#16, 2000 NB) saw 17-19 V with grounds already checked and the alternator bench-tested at 15.1 V. So the ground-offset cause is not general, and the thread's cause is a vendor-specific regulator circuit.
- "Lean stalls traced to base-map battery-voltage correction": only 107805 supports it (the 0.2 ms/V figure comes from the audit's notes, not checked).
- This design: the 99-00 ECU drives a logic-level PWM wire into the alternator's internal regulator (VERIFICATION M5). Control ships off, so the stock regulator governs. The vendor's mechanism does not transfer directly. A ground offset could still in principle upset the alternator's own regulator, but that is speculation and no source shows it.
(a) Already documented: "alternator control ships off" is in review brief section 6, README:100 and INSTALL:253. The ground-strap advice and an over-voltage cutoff are not in the repo (no "strap" hit), so they are new but low value.
(b) Ordered boards: hardware is unaffected. This is a doc/firmware default only. Not a rework item.
Corrected statement: Two forum threads report 99-00 MSPNP2 overcharge. One vendor engineer attributes it second-hand to alternator ground offset; another case was a vendor regulator misset. Both concern the vendor's own regulator circuit. This repo ships alternator control off, so the risk applies only after the user enables it.
Severity: low/info. Fix: add one INSTALL line (check the alternator ground strap; watch battery voltage on first enable), optionally a firmware over-voltage cutoff.
