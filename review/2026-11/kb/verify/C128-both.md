# C128 verdict: partly (technically sound, benefit smaller than stated, not ordered boards)
Checked:
- ZXMS6004FF datasheet (datasheets/extra/ZXMS6004FF_diodes.txt l.140-160, 241-255): VIH 3 V min, VIL 0.7 V max, Rds 400 typ / 600 mohm max at VIN 3 V, ID(LIM) 0.7 A min at 3 V, IIN 100 uA max at 3 V. TRUE. Hottest load is 402 mA, so 3 V drive is adequate (current limit 0.7 A min vs 1 A min at 5 V, still above load).
- design.py:268-296: CEL already driven from MCU via R160 1k (rev 0.10). The other six _CTL lines already exist as MCU nets, so no pin-count change. U12 is a TSSOP-24 SN74LVC4245A (C7859), plus C64/C65 saved. TRUE.
- Price: parts_manifest.md l.48 gives 0.5334 / 0.3207 (two tiers) for C7859; "$0.39 at 30" not re-derived, but saving is roughly $0.32-0.39 plus two 0402 caps. Small.
- Cost of the change (the claim understates it): the shared DRV_EN_N hardware gate protects against a released control pin (rusEFI parks released pins as input pull-UP, board_configuration.cpp l.48-60). Without U12, a pull-up (30-50 k) against 10 k pull-down + 1 k gives about 0.65-0.8 V at the gate, i.e. at/above VIN(TH) min 0.7 V, so a released pin could partly turn on a relay/FP during rusEFI re-apply (config Burn). Today the enable is dropped in that window (board_configuration.cpp l.116-127). Firmware parking (pull-down) covers it only if run first; this loses the hardware backstop for FP, MAINRLY, FAN, AC, TACH, LS7. In a fatal error the CEL path already is handled in firmware; the other relays rely on rusEFI turning pins off.
- Reset/boot: pins float, 10 k pulldowns hold off. OK.
Not already documented as an open item (review brief s6): no. VERIFICATION.md H-rows describe U12 as design; CEL bypass is in rev0.10 audit.
Ordered boards: NOT affected as a rework item. ord_db (rev 0.9) has U12 (footprints csv); removing it is a design change for the next rev only (and would need the tie of nets, not hand-reworkable cheaply).
Corrected statement: Dropping U12 is electrically feasible (ZXMS6004FF fully on from 3 V, CEL already does it) and saves one TSSOP-24 (~$0.32-0.39) and two caps, but removes the hardware enable that holds the six relay/tach/LS7 drivers off while rusEFI releases pins (pull-up park state gives gate voltage near the 0.7 V min threshold). Saving is marginal against loss of a deliberate safety backstop.
Fix: optional for rev next; if done, add MCU-side pull-down logic validated on hardware or keep U12; value is low. Recommend low severity.
