# B15 - Audit of VERIFICATION.md claims (what the design's safety really rests on)

STATUS: complete (2026-11-16). Scratch: work/B15-verification-claims-audit/ (repo_copy, out_inj2, out_cs, out_full, own/, shim/, changes.txt)

## Summary
1. VERIFICATION.md is a desk-and-simulation report. Every number comes from a hand-written model (ngspice decks, a DC fault solver, a finite-difference thermal model, a Renode platform with ~13 self-written peripheral models, a PC model of the F103 ROM bootloader). Nothing is measured; nothing was cross-checked against a second, independent tool except stm32flash against the author's own bootloader model.
2. Independent re-runs reproduce the repo's headline numbers exactly: sim_inj_clamp.py (all rows, 8.78 mJ / 31.9 V / worst 35.7 V), circuit_sim.py (0 FAIL; load dump 34.2 / 36.4 / 23.5 V), circuit_sim_full.py (93 OK / 11 CHECK / 1 REF / 0 FAIL, identical to L1069), board_checks.py (1602 OK / 265 CHECK / 4 FAIL, identical to L1069). So the scripts are honest about what they compute. What they compute is only as good as the assumed parameters.
3. Weak spots found: (a) energy absorbed by input TVS D6 in the suppressed load dump is never reported; my deck gives 4.8 J (D6 at max Vbr) to 17.9 J (min Vbr, ~45 W for 400 ms), a regime the SMCJ30A datasheet curves (end at 10 ms) do not cover; (b) the +3V3 shunt clamp's 70 mV margin at 1 A is fragile (my deck: 4.2-4.9 V with plausible X5R derating / TL431 bandwidth), though the realistic fault current is below 0.4 A; (c) all temperatures rest on h = 10 W/m2K both faces plus an unspecified "air inside the case"; (d) reset-state safety is netlist reasoning only, and the one item the report itself says is not tested (enable timing) is the item the claim rests on; (e) wideband flashing and "firmware runs every year" are emulation against models written by the same author, not silicon.
4. Stale / contradictory text in VERIFICATION.md: the Safety review table and the 1989-93 paragraph still describe rev 0.6 (two drivers per wire, 0.99 A, SMCJ40A, ULN2003 relays) while the head of the file and L1015-1069 say one driver per wire with 2.3 A and SMCJ30A; the Summary bullet still says "two drivers per injector wire, with a clamp on each wire"; circuit_sim.py (the older script) still models SMCJ40A/NCV8406A parts; the cited "84 OK / 12 CHECK" at L560/606 is superseded by 93/11 at L1069; load-dump energy is stated for D12 (0 mJ) which is the irrelevant device.
5. Nothing found that disproves a claim outright. Findings are about weight the evidence can bear.

## Findings detail

### F1 stale/contradictory text (doc)
- L61-63 (Summary): "The 1989-93 board now uses two drivers per injector wire, with a clamp on each wire". Superseded by L65-72 and L1015-1069 (one driver per wire, no shunts, 2.3 A, batch). The ordered/current motherboard footprints: cur_mb8995 has D3/D4 SMCJ30A and JP1/JP2, ord_mb8993 has D3/D4 SMCJ30A and R23/R24 0R (evidence footprints csv).
- L1150 relay load "about 0.18 A each through the ULN2003"; L1109 (M6) says the ULN2003 is gone since rev 0.8.
- L1196-1206 "1989-93 paired injectors ... Rev 0.6 halves the current ... 0.99 A per driver ... The two SMCJ40A take about 1 W": the board has SMCJ30A (L568), and L1015+ reverts to one driver per wire with ~2.3 A. The "Before rev 0.6 risk moderate; rev 0.6 removes the risk" paragraph is the opposite of the current state on 1989-93 cars (the hottest case per L65-72).
- L1167 table row "Injectors ... stock-wired 1989-93: two per driver, about 2.3 A" contradicts the next paragraph's 0.99 A in the same section.
- L560/L606 "84 OK / 12 CHECK" vs L1069 "93 OK / 11 CHECK" (both appear; my run gives 93/11/1/0).
- L42-43 Summary "firmware works in simulation for all five years" while L1073 admits the final change was not re-emulated.
- circuit_sim.py (older script, 20 OK / 3 CHECK / 6 REF) still models SMCJ40A (line 270) and NCV8406A (227); VERIFICATION cites it for "as built" load dump and relay results; these sections are valid only because the load-dump deck is independent of those parts.
- tools: circuit_sim_full.py tvs_copper_area() looks for motherboards/1989-93/miata_mb_8993.kicad_pcb which does not exist in the current repo (the directory is 1989-95); the function silently returns 0 area. In the unmodified repo that path is exception-guarded; in my copy I only added an assert so a missing board falls back the same way. Effect: the "TVS copper area" the model uses for D3/D4 may be the default, not the real islands (not traced further).

### F2 D6 energy in a suppressed load dump never reported (risk, INFERRED/VERIFIED model result)
VERIFICATION reports only the energy into D12 (0 mJ). The 400 ms 35 V / 0.5 ohm dump is absorbed by D6 (SMCJ30A, Vbr 33.3-36.8 V). My deck (own/ld_*.cir; D6 rs fitted to Vc 48.4 V at 31 A: 0.49 ohm at min Vbr, 0.37 ohm at max): D6 energy 4.8 J (Vbr 36.8) to 17.9 J (Vbr 33.3), average ~45 W for 400 ms at the low end. Littelfuse DS Figure 2 (peak pulse power vs pulse width) stops at 10 ms; the SMCJ rating is 1.5 kW at 10/1000 us (about 1.5 J). A 400 ms avalanche pulse is outside the datasheet; a square-root-of-time derating from 1.5 kW gives ~75 W allowed at 400 ms, so margin ~1.7x on that rule of thumb (UNVERIFIED rule). ISO 16750-2 says Ri 0.5-4 ohm; at Ri 0.5 it is worst. If the Miata alternator is unsuppressed (79-101 V, Ri 0.5-4 ohm; the repo lists the level as unknown), D6 would see ~330 J in the same deck (80 V, 2 ohm) and fail short (the car's fuse then blows); +12V stays at 42 V peak in that deck (below 44 V only by luck of D6 at its max Vbr). Worth stating in the doc.
