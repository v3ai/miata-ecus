# Next batch of boards: findings and next steps

Written 2026-11-17 from the completed review (`REPORT.md`) and four follow-up investigations (`kb/followup/`). "Next batch" means the first order placed after the prototypes now at the fab have been brought up: a daughterboard revision after rev 0.10 (called rev 0.11 here) and the motherboards that go with it. Prices and stock are as of 2026-11-16/17 and move daily.

Confidence marks: **[C]** confirmed by adversarial verification or checked directly in the design files; **[P]** partly: a verifier narrowed it and the narrowed version is what is stated; **[S]** single source, not independently verified; **[M]** needs a measurement on a board or a car.

## 2. Next steps, in order

### Step 1. Bring up the ordered boards (gate for everything else)

Follow `REPORT.md` section S1 Part 1. The essentials:

1. Replace R110 with a 62 Ω 1% 1206 resistor (C17971) on every daughterboard.
2. Check the JLCPCB order record for the via-covering option. On the boards themselves, open holes in any unpopulated SMD pad mean the vias were not filled. If they were not, inspect the Q1-Q8 tab joints, the exposed pads of U2, U3 and U14, and the clamp diodes before power.
3. Check orientation of the SOT-23 diodes, each motherboard's USB protection part, and D6, D7, D12.
4. On 1989-95 boards set the tach output pin to "none" in the ordered firmware before the board meets a car.
5. Run the staged bench sequence (current-limited rails, flash, outputs on dummy loads, wideband in free air), then one car.

### Step 7. Ordering the batch

1. **Order a small pilot of rev 0.11 first** (5 daughterboards, roughly $85 each on the cost model). This differs from the report's cost section, which goes straight to 30: rev 0.11 will carry a new via rule, a re-route, a new processor part number and every rev 0.10 change that rev 0.9 could not test. A layout fault found after a lot of 30 costs over $2,000.
2. Then one daughterboard lot of about 30 for all years, on JLCPCB Economic assembly (the limit is 50 boards). One run for all years saves $18-25 per set against per-year runs.
3. Motherboards per validated year, 8-10 each. Do not order 1996-2005 until a real case has been measured.
4. On the order page: 6 layers, via covering as chosen in step 3, check the placement preview for rotations, use Combine Shipping, and read the duty line (JLCPCB charges a flat 35%; whether it applies to parts it buys is not yet known).
5. Check stock on the day: processor, 40-pin sockets (buy the whole run's worth at once), MAP sensors, TJA1042, C960026.
6. Buy harness headers in lots of 12-15 per year. Confirm each part against the board footprint first: distributors describe the pitch of 174917-6 differently (3.5 mm and 4.5 mm), and stock figures are aggregator data. Buy 176122-6 only for the ordered 1999-2000 boards.
7. Budget: about $153 per set at 30; about $3,400 cash for 30 board sets, $7,400 if they are sold as complete kits.

## 3. Decisions that are yours

1. **Processor:** move to STM32F405VGT6 (recommended), or keep the F407 and buy it through JLCPCB global sourcing (9-20 business days; price not visible without an account).
2. **Wideband lead:** design around the AEM 30-3427 harness ($32 retail, single source, fit untested) or a seller-made lead (about $19 in parts; the install guide must change).
3. **External IAT:** document the plug-in adapter harnesses ($45, 1990-97 only; none found for 1999-2005) or restore a header.
4. **Injector driver swap:** worth $8.80 per set, after a bench test.
5. **MAP sensor:** keep the 700 kPa through-hole part or accept a new footprint and 400 kPa for $17.
6. **VR-sensor conditioner:** fit or leave off by default.
